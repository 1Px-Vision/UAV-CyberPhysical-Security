# UAS Law, Ethics, Regulation, Legislation, and Adjudication
Apply standards based on their scope and the specific organizational or technical issue. ISO/IEC 27001 focuses on information security management, and ISO/IEC 42001 covers AI management systems. These standards can guide governance discussions, but they do not replace UAS-specific engineering assessments or operational checks. For each selected standard, record its edition, applicable requirements, implementation responsibilities, and supporting evidence. Treat standards alignment, proof of conformity, certification, and legal compliance as separate claims. Assign responsibilities to developers, integrators, operators, maintainers, and decision-makers, and retain records for future review.

## Practical Example: Evaluating an ISO Compliance Claim
An organization's statement that its AI-enabled UAS security system is “ISO compliant” requires clarification before it can support an assurance decision. Identify the specific standard, the claim's subject and scope, the activities assessed, and the evidence linking applicable requirements to the conclusion. This exercise concerns evidence appraisal. Check conclusions about particular requirements against the identified standard and its applicable edition.

### Practical Exercise 15.1: Reviewing an ISO Compliance Claim
**Objective:** Determine what an organization's compliance claim supports by examining applicability, assessed scope, assigned responsibilities, requirement traceability, audit records, and unresolved gaps.

**Worked Situation:** An organization describes its AI-enabled UAS security system as “ISO compliant.” You are asked to review the claim before using it in an assurance report or procurement decision. The initial statement does not identify a standard, edition, assessed activities, or supporting evidence. Request and evaluate supporting records without inferring these details.

**Practical Procedure:** Preserve the claim. Record its exact wording, source, publication date, and the system or organizational activity it appears to refer to.

**Identify the standard:** Obtain its full designation, title, edition, and relevant amendments. Consult the authoritative text before interpreting requirements.

**Establish the scope:** Identify the organizational unit, location, service, process, system version, operational activity, and relevant exclusions or boundaries.

**Determine the assessment basis:** Establish whether the claim rests on an internal assessment, supplier declaration, external audit, certificate, or another documented assessment. Record who performed it and the conclusion actually issued.

**Trace requirements to evidence:** Map applicable requirements to responsible roles, implementation records, verification evidence, and audit findings. Identify missing links and unresolved findings.

**Issue a bounded conclusion:** State what the evidence supports, what it does not establish, and what further verification is required.

>Hint: Keep the assessment subject explicit. Evidence addressing an organizational management process must not automatically be extended to every product function, deployment, or AI-model version. A technical test result supports only the properties, configuration, conditions, and acceptance criteria actually evaluated. Read the scope and assessment conclusion before interpreting a logo, certificate title, or marketing statement.

Worked Solution: 
1. Clarify the Claim and Its Supporting Records Start by noting that the unqualified statement is insufficiently specific for a conformity conclusion.
2. Request: The exact standard designation and edition.
* A written scope statement.
* The assessed activities.
* The records supporting the claim.
Where a certificate is supplied, examine the named holder, issuer, scope, dates, status, and referenced assessment scheme. Verify authenticity and status through an authoritative issuer or registry where available. A certificate image alone does not establish current coverage.
3. Build a Requirement-to-Evidence Matrix
For every applicable requirement, record its identifier, applicability rationale, responsible role, implementation reference, verification or audit evidence, finding, and unresolved action.
Field	Information to Record:
- Requirement	Identifier in the identified standard and edition
- Applicability	Rationale, assessed subject, scope, and relevant boundaries
- Responsibility	Accountable role and evidence provider
- Implementation	Versioned implementation or process reference
- Evidence	Evidence identifier, verification method, and audit reference
- Finding	Supported within scope, unsupported, or unresolved
- Follow-up	Unresolved action, closure owner, target date, and decision effect. Where a requirement is treated as outside scope, document the basis and confirm that this treatment is permitted by the identified requirements and assessment arrangement.
4. Review Coverage of the Claimed AI Security Activities
Relevant evidence may include:
- Documented responsibilities and risk assessments.
- Configuration control and model and data versions.
- Validation results and monitoring procedures.
- Human oversight records.
- Incident handling and corrective-action records.
- These are candidate evidence categories. They do not replace the requirements of the identified standard.
5. Conclude Within the Verified Scope
If evidence supports an assessed organizational process, describe that process and its boundaries. If system-specific technical assurance is also claimed, require evidence addressing the relevant technical properties. Do not infer attack resistance, predictive accuracy, operational safety, or mission suitability from an unspecified compliance statement.

**Assessment Record**
Create one record per reviewed requirement or claim element.

**Category	Required Record:** Applicability	Standard and edition, requirement identifier, assessed subject, scope, and rationale
Responsibility	Accountable owner, evidence provider, reviewer, and decision authority
Traceability	Implementation reference, relevant system or model version, evidence identifier, and verification method
Audit records	Assessor identity, assessment date, assessed activities, findings, and corrective-action references
Unresolved gaps	Missing or outdated evidence, scope ambiguity, open findings, closure owner, target date, and effect on the decision
Decision and Reporting

**Classify each reviewed claim element as:** Supported within a stated scope. The reviewed evidence supports a precisely bounded conclusion.
Unsupported. The reviewed evidence does not support the stated claim.
Unresolved pending additional evidence. Missing information prevents a conclusion.
Distinguish an absence of evidence from demonstrated failure to meet a requirement. Retain the reason for each judgment and the reviewer's identity.

If the organization provides no identified standard or assessment records, report:
The statement is insufficiently specified to determine conformity. The standard, edition, assessed scope, and supporting evidence remain unverified.
If adequate evidence is subsequently provided, replace this statement with a precise description of the supported conclusion and its limitations, rather than retaining the unqualified phrase “ISO compliant.”

Implementation Checklist:
- [ ] Applicability: Identify the exact standard, edition, assessed subject, activities, and scope boundaries.
- [ ] Responsibility: Assign evidence owners, reviewers, and acceptance authority.
- [ ] Traceability: Connect each applicable requirement to versioned implementation and verification evidence.
- [ ] Audit records: Examine assessment dates, findings, certificate scope where relevant, and corrective-action status.
- [ ] Unresolved gaps: Record missing evidence, open findings, owners, deadlines, and decision implications.
- [ ] Verify that the final wording does not extend beyond the supported scope.

**Expected Deliverable:** A claim-assessment report containing the identified standard and scope, a requirement-to-evidence matrix, responsibility assignments, reviewed audit records, unresolved gaps, and a precisely bounded conclusion about what the claim supports.
